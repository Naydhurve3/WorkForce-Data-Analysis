INTERACTION IMPACT SCORE
========================

The Interaction Impact Score is a custom metric that quantifies the strength
of a pairwise feature interaction for a given workforce outcome.

Formula:
    Impact(X, Y -> O) = I(X;Y) * |delta_O| * log(n_segment)

Where:
    I(X;Y)       = Mutual Information between features X and Y
                   = sum_x sum_y p(x,y) * log( p(x,y) / (p(x) * p(y)) )

    |delta_O|    = Absolute difference in outcome mean between the interaction
                   segment and the population baseline
                   = |mean(O | X=x, Y=y) - mean(O)|

    log(n_segment) = Logarithmic weight for segment size (penalizes very small
                     groups, rewards well-supported interactions)

Interpretation:
    Higher impact scores indicate stronger interaction effects. The score
    balances statistical dependence (MI), practical significance (delta),
    and reliability (sample size).

    Top score in our study: 181.3 (JobFamily x DepartmentType -> PayZone)


STATISTICAL TESTS
=================

For each pair of features, we apply the appropriate statistical test based
on their types:

1. Categorical x Categorical:
       Chi-square test of independence
       chi2 = sum (O - E)^2 / E
       where O = observed frequency, E = expected frequency

2. Categorical x Numeric:
       ANOVA F-test (one-way)
       F = MS_between / MS_within

3. Numeric x Numeric:
       Pearson correlation coefficient
       r = cov(X, Y) / (sigma_X * sigma_Y)

Multiple comparison correction: Bonferroni (alpha / n_tests)


COHEN'S D (Effect Size)
=======================

Used in deep dives to measure the standardized difference between a segment
and the population:

    d = (mu_segment - mu_rest) / s_pooled

    s_pooled = sqrt(((n1 - 1) * s1^2 + (n2 - 1) * s2^2) / (n1 + n2 - 2))

Interpretation:
    d = 0.2   Small effect
    d = 0.5   Medium effect
    d = 0.8   Large effect


MUTUAL INFORMATION
==================

    I(X;Y) = sum_x sum_y p(x,y) * log( p(x,y) / (p(x) * p(y)) )

Measures the reduction in uncertainty about Y given knowledge of X.
Range: 0 (independent) to min(H(X), H(Y)) (fully dependent).
